"healthy food" is nearly always context dependent - and my definition of healthy food is this: _ Food is "healthy" if it gives you more of the nutrients your body is in need of (at that point in time), whilst giving little of the nutrients or toxins that your body doesn't need - it will be beneficial to your health to eat that food. _ Here, we have the McDonald's burgers. Actually, beef is one of the most nutritious foods you can eat! High in many micronutrients (and most of us tend to be short of a few micronutrients even on a good wholefoods diet), and of course high in protein. I think most people would be healthier if they ate more protein. _ However, with the burgers, I don't have the fries or bun, and in this case, I have a low-carb protein-bread that I made in around 7 minutes including 5 mins cooking time! _ This makes this far healthier for me because I have a starch intolerance so cannot eat wheat or potatoes. My bread is also high in fibre which is probably good for gut health. _ There is also some sauerkraut mixed with "Lighter than Light" mayonnaise - there is already nutritious fat from the beef, so the omega 6 oils in regular mayonnaise would simply be a source of excess calories, plus too much omega 6 isn't that good for you. The sauerkraut has a range of micronutrients as well as being good for the gut due to the vegetable fibres, whilst containing few calories. _ And obviously the tomatoes are good too. _ So in short, this meal supplies lots of high quality protein and micronutrients, with some nice saturated fats to fuel my training later (but avoiding piling on additional unnecessary fats from carbs or mayonnaise). The meal was very healthy because it contained almost entirely things my body needed with very little that it didn't. _ I do have some carbs in my diet but probably only around 100g a day, from fruit, veg, some chocolate and glucose. I personally don't feel good with much more carbs than this.
